How it works

Three moves, every session.

No chat, no small talk, no camera filters. Just the quiet pressure of other people also doing the work.

First

Drop anchor

Pick a slot. You're placed in a room with two to four others starting the same kind of work block — writing, code, admin, reading.

Then

Work the tide

Camera on, mic off. A shared timer counts the block down — 25 or 50 minutes. You see the room; the room sees you. That's the entire mechanism.

Last

Log the session

Sixty seconds at the end: say out loud what you did. It's added to your log and your streak — a record you can actually look back on.

Inside a room

What you actually see

A timer, a handful of faces, and how many minutes are left in the block. No feed, no reactions, no notifications — the room is built to be forgettable so the work isn't.

Questions

Before you join a room.

Do I need my camera on?

Yes. Camera on, mic off — that's the format. It's what makes the room work; without it, it's just a countdown timer in a tab.

What if nobody else is in my room?

We hold a minimum crew of two. If your room is short, we merge it into the next one starting within five minutes.

Is this only for freelancers?

No — students make up about a third of crews. Anyone doing solo, unsupervised work fits: writing, coding, reading, admin, studying.

What time zone do rooms run in?

All of them. Rooms open around the clock in 25- and 50-minute blocks, so there's almost always one starting soon.
